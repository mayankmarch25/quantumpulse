The maths you need

Part I of Dancing with Qubits, in {{ site.data.tracks.maths | size }} topics. None of them needs more than school algebra to start. The editions cover them in the book’s order, chapters 2 to 6, so the list below is the reading order. Every maths edition ends by showing where that piece turns up in a qubit.

The one rule to carry everywhere: a qubit is described by numbers called amplitudes, and the probability of a result is the amplitude squared. Every other topic on this page exists to keep that rule working as the system gets bigger.

If you remember nothing else from the maths track, remember that squaring is why amplitudes can be negative, and negative amplitudes are why quantum computers can do anything a coin cannot.
